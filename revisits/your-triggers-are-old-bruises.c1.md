One way to find a bruise is to notice what does not hurt. If a stranger calls you a purple giraffe, you laugh. It bounces off, because there is nothing sore underneath. But if someone suggests you are lazy, or selfish, or a fraud at your job, and it lands like a slap, part of you may already fear it could be true.

Some bruises are not even yours. A father flares up at any sign of disrespect, and thirty years later his son does the same, often while swearing he is nothing like his father. A reaction you were handed feels like your nature. Seen as something learned, it becomes something you can question.

One balance, though. Not every strong reaction is only an old bruise. Ask two questions: what in me got touched, and what in the situation might genuinely need to change? [The trigger behind the trigger](/blog/the-trigger-behind-the-trigger/).

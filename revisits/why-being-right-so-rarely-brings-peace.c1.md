There is a reason winning the point feels so hollow. In a heated argument, the mind can behave less like a judge weighing the facts and more like a lawyer. It has already picked its side, and it is brilliant at building the case: every point in your favor found, every point against waved away. The other person's mind is doing exactly the same.

So a won argument does not mean you found the truth. Sometimes it only means you made the stronger case that day.

And the other person is keeping a tab, just not the one you think. A week later almost no one remembers the clever point. What they may remember most is how you made them feel: small, steamrolled, talked down to. That is what goes on the tab, and warmth, once it drains out, is slow to refill. [We are lawyers, not judges](/blog/we-are-lawyers-not-judges/).

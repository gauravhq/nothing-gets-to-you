There is a useful way to tell the two apart: swallowing versus digesting.

Swallowing is what the fearful silence does. You feel the anger and shove it down whole, unlooked at. It does not leave. It tends to surface later, at the worst moment, aimed at the wrong person. The partner who never says what bothers them carries a heavier and heavier bag, until a dish left in the sink tips it over and years come out at once. It was never about the dish.

Digesting is different. You look at the feeling, name it, let it move through you, and only then decide it does not need to become words. One more check keeps a silence honest: give it a return point. A pause with no return point can quietly turn into avoidance wearing the clothes of calm.

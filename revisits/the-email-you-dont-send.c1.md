My own version of this rule is simple. I write the whole angry reply, every sharp line of it, and then I leave it in drafts overnight.

Most mornings I read it back, feel relieved that no one else saw it, and delete it. Occasionally the issue still needs a response, but the next-day version is almost always shorter and more useful. Whatever felt so urgent at ten at night has usually shrunk by morning.

The rule works because it was made on a calm afternoon, not in the heat. You are not counting on being wise in your worst moment. You are letting your best moment, the calm one where you made the rule, reach forward and protect you. A few others are worth deciding in advance: sleep on anything that feels urgent after nine at night, and when your voice starts to rise, say you need a minute, and take it.

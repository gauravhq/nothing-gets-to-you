Look at the exchange rate. A few minutes of reacting can cost a whole evening of peace. Few of us would sign that deal if it were written on paper and slid across a desk. We agree to it anyway, because the flash comes first and feels like a bargain, and the hours after feel like weather that just happens to us.

It is not weather. Much of the tail is us, relighting the fire. Part of that is habit, a groove the replay runs in on its own. Part of it, if we are honest, is that being the wronged one hands us something: a little certainty, a story in which we came out ahead. Letting it go means setting that story down.

The other person has most likely moved on. Notice when you are the only one still working on a fight that ended hours ago. [The replay tax](/blog/the-replay-tax/).

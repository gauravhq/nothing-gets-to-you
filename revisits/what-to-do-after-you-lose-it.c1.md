Two things about the repair are easy to get wrong.

First, you control the offering, not how it is received. Sometimes you will own it cleanly and the other person will stay cold, or bring it up again tomorrow. The old pull will rise to say: I already apologized, what more do you want? Do not. That is another reaction, quietly undoing the repair. Offer it once, mean it, and let them take their own time. Chasing their forgiveness turns an apology into a demand.

Second, there is a ditch on the other side of the road. Groveling, apologizing over and over, asks the person you hurt to reassure you. That is not humility. It is the ego again, in a costume of guilt. And when the same behavior keeps returning, the apology is not the whole repair. The change they can eventually see is. [The apology that asks nothing](/blog/the-apology-that-asks-nothing/).

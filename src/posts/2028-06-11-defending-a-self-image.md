---
title: "Defending a self-image"
date: 2028-06-11
pageDescription: "When you get defensive, you are usually not protecting a fact. You are protecting a picture of yourself."
---

When you get defensive, notice what you are actually defending. Usually it is not a fact. It is a picture of yourself.

Someone suggests you were careless, or wrong, or unfair, and you push back hard. But the heat is not really about the specific claim. It is about protecting an image, the competent one, the good one, the one who does not make that kind of mistake. The claim threatened the picture, so you defended the picture.

Seeing this helps, because the picture is not actually you, and it does not need armor. You can be someone who was careless this once and still be fundamentally fine. The image can take a dent without you falling apart.

So when defensiveness flares, ask what you are guarding. If it is a self-image, you can loosen your grip. You are more than the picture, and you do not have to fight everyone who smudges it.

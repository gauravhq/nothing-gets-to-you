---
title: "The wrong fact that doesn't matter"
date: 2028-11-12
pageDescription: "Before you correct a mistaken fact, ask one thing: does it actually matter? Most of them do not."
---

Someone states a fact, and it is wrong, and you know it is wrong. The urge to correct is immediate. Pause on one question first. Does it matter?

So much of what we itch to correct changes nothing. The wrong date, the misremembered name, the slightly off figure in a casual chat. Being right about it would not help anyone. It would just interrupt, and make the moment briefly about you knowing better.

There is a difference between a wrong fact that matters, one that will lead to a bad decision or real harm, and a wrong fact that is simply floating by. The first is worth a gentle word. The second you can let sail past.

So before you correct, sort it. Does this actually matter, or do I just want to be right? If it does not matter, let it go. Most wrong facts are harmless, and most corrections cost more calm than the accuracy is worth.

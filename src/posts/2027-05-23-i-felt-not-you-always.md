---
title: "I felt, not you always"
date: 2027-05-23
pageDescription: "How you say the hard thing decides whether it gets heard at all."
---

When you do speak up about something that bothered you, how you say it decides whether it gets heard.

There is a small shift that changes everything. Instead of you always, or you never, or you are wrong, say I felt, or I see it differently, or here is how that landed for me.

The first kind puts the other person on trial, and people on trial defend, they do not listen. The second kind describes your own experience, which is hard to argue with and easy to hear.

This is not about being soft. You can say something firm and clear this way. You are just removing the accusation that would have made them stop listening before you finished.

Speak from your own side of the line. Say how it felt, what you saw, what you need. It is the difference between being right and actually being heard.

---
title: "The need to be seen as smart"
date: 2024-09-08
pageDescription: "Half of what we say in a conversation is the ego reaching for standing, not actually adding anything."
---

Watch a conversation closely and you will often catch it, the quiet need to be seen as smart. To have the clever take, the correction, the fact nobody else knew.

It feels like sharing. Often it is the ego steering. You are not adding to the conversation so much as adding to your standing in it. And people can feel the difference, even if they cannot name it.

This is worth catching, because the need to look smart makes you a worse listener and a tenser talker. You are half in the room and half rehearsing your next impressive line.

So notice when the urge shows up. Am I saying this to help, or to look good? You do not have to shame it. Just seeing it loosens its grip. And oddly, the person who is not trying to look smart usually comes across as the wisest one there.

---
title: "The urge to correct a detail"
date: 2028-07-02
pageDescription: "The itch to fix a small wrong detail in someone's story is usually the ego, not accuracy. Let it stand."
---

Someone gets a small detail wrong in their story. The wrong year, the wrong name, the slightly off fact. And you feel it, the itch to correct them.

Most of the time, that itch is the ego, not accuracy. The detail does not matter to the story. Correcting it does nothing except interrupt them and quietly make the moment about you being precise. But the pull is strong, because being right, even about a tiny thing, feels good.

This is worth catching, because these little corrections add up. They tell people you are keeping score of their errors, and they make you tiring to talk to.

So let the small wrong detail stand. It was the wrong year, and it does not matter. The story is still the story. Learning to let tiny inaccuracies pass, when nothing important rests on them, is a small daily way of getting the ego out of the driver's seat.

---
title: "Correcting pushes people away"
date: 2028-11-26
pageDescription: "Even accurate corrections often leave a person feeling smaller, not more informed. Warmth is the hidden cost."
---

We correct people thinking it helps them. Usually it just pushes them away.

Being corrected, even gently, rarely feels good. It feels like being told you are less, that you got it wrong, that someone was watching for your mistake. So even when your correction is accurate, the person often walks away feeling worse about you, not more informed.

This is worth knowing, because it means correcting has a cost we forget to count. You might win accuracy and lose warmth. You might be right and be a little less liked for it.

None of this means never correct anyone. It means correct far less than the urge demands, and only when it truly matters. For everything else, letting it go keeps the connection intact. People remember how you made them feel more than the facts you handed them, and constant correcting makes them feel small.

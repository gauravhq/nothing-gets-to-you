There is a quiet practice that makes you harder to provoke. Noticing what is good.

When your attention is trained on what is wrong, what is missing, what is unfair, you walk around primed to react, already half-annoyed, ready to catch the next slight. But regularly noticing what is good, a few things each day, slowly shifts the baseline. You start from a fuller, warmer place, and from there the small provocations do not land as hard.

This is not about pretending everything is fine or ignoring real problems. It is about not letting your attention rest only on the negative, which is where it drifts by default.

So build in a small habit of noticing. A few good things at the day's end. A moment of appreciation for something ordinary. It sounds soft, but it works on the nervous system, lowering the fuse over time. A mind that regularly notices the good is a mind that reacts less to the bad, because it is not standing on empty, waiting for the next thing to go wrong.

---
title: "The slow yes and the slow no"
date: 2029-04-29
pageDescription: "We blurt yes to please and snap no to defend. A considered answer is one you can actually stand behind."
---

Two of the most useful responses you can build are the slow yes and the slow no.

We rush both. We blurt yes to things we do not really want, to please, to avoid the awkward pause. We snap no out of defensiveness, before we have even considered. Both are reactions, and both get us into trouble.

The slow version fixes it. Let me think about that. Give me a day. A considered yes is one you will actually honor, given freely, not squeezed out of you. A considered no is one you can stand behind, calm and clear, not a reflexive shove.

So when a request or a choice lands, resist the fast answer, even the fast yes. Slow it down. The pause is not rude. It shows you take the thing seriously enough to actually think. And the yes or no that comes out of a slow, clear head is one you will rarely regret, which is more than the fast ones can promise.

---
title: "The apology that asks nothing"
date: 2029-10-28
pageDescription: "A real apology takes responsibility and leaves the other person free. It does not fish for reassurance."
---

There is a kind of apology that heals, and a kind that quietly asks for something. Learn to tell them apart.

The healing kind asks nothing. I was wrong. I am sorry. That is it. It takes full responsibility and leaves the other person free, with no pressure to respond, to reassure, to forgive on the spot.

The other kind is really a request in disguise. It goes on too long, explains too much, and somehow ends with the other person comforting you, telling you it is okay, managing your guilt. That is not an apology. That is asking them to carry your feelings about the thing you did.

So when you apologize, keep it clean and let it ask for nothing. Say the true thing, take the responsibility, and then be quiet. Do not fish for reassurance. Do not make them do the emotional work. A real apology is a gift you give, not a favor you request. The clean, quiet kind is the one that actually mends things.

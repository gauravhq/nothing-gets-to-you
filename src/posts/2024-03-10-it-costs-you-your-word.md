---
title: "It costs you your word"
date: 2024-03-10
pageDescription: "Reacting makes you say things you do not mean, and people quietly weigh everything you say a little less."
---

When you react, you say things you do not mean. And once they are said, you cannot fully take them back.

The cruel line in the argument. The exaggeration thrown to win. The blanket you always, you never. In the heat it felt true, or at least useful. Later you know it was neither. But the other person heard it, and part of them files it away.

This is a quiet cost of reacting. It spends your credibility. If your words in anger are wild and unfair, people start to weigh everything you say a little less, even the true things.

Steady words carry more. When you speak from calm, people trust that you mean what you say, because you are not known for hurling things you will later regret. Guarding your reactions is also guarding your word, and your word is worth more than any point you win by breaking it.

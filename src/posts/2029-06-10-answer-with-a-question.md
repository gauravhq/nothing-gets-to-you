---
title: "Answer with a question"
date: 2029-06-10
pageDescription: "A real question buys a pause and turns the other person from attacking to explaining. When in doubt, ask."
---

When something provokes you, the reflex is to answer with a statement, usually a sharp one. A stronger response is often to answer with a question.

Someone makes an accusation, or a jab, or a sweeping claim. Instead of firing back, you ask. What makes you say that? Can you tell me more about what is bothering you? A question does two things a comeback cannot. It buys you a pause, and it turns the other person from attacking to explaining.

Often the question alone deflates the whole thing. Asked to unpack the jab, the person softens, or realizes they overstated, or reveals what is actually wrong underneath, which is usually not what they first said.

So keep a few honest questions ready for hot moments. Not sarcastic ones, real ones, asked with genuine curiosity. They keep you calm, they slow the exchange, and they often uncover the real issue faster than any argument would. When in doubt, ask before you assert.

---
title: "Name what you see"
date: 2029-05-13
pageDescription: "Describing what is happening is much harder to fight than accusing the person. Describe, do not accuse."
---

Here is a response that lowers heat instead of raising it. Instead of attacking, name what you see.

Rather than you are being unfair, you shout at me, try, it feels like this got heated, or, I think we are both frustrated. You are describing the situation rather than accusing the person. And description is much harder to fight than accusation.

When you attack, the other person defends, and you are off. When you simply name what is happening, calmly, it often stops the spiral. It brings a moment of shared awareness. Oh. Yes. This did get heated.

This takes practice, because the reflex is to accuse. But naming what you see keeps you out of the blame game and puts you both on the same side, looking at the problem together instead of at each other. Describe, do not accuse. It is a small shift in words that makes a large difference in where the conversation goes next.
